## Abstracts

1. **GCP DPIA (2026).** Assesses four core Google Cloud services for Dutch central-government use against GDPR. Distinguishes Content, Account, Diagnostic, Support and Website data; examines processor/controller roles, residency, encryption and confidential computing, transfers and retention. Finds 11 low risks subject to stated measures.
2. **CMS on FTC independence.** Explains the Supreme Court's expansion of presidential removal power over FTC commissioners and why that matters for the DPF and the UK–US Data Bridge, with recommendations to reassess transfer risk mitigation.
3. **BMJ Digital Health.** Argues that moving AI-enabled medical devices from Annex I Section A to Section B of the AI Act would retain the high-risk label but remove most AI-specific safeguards, relying on MDR/IVDR that lack equivalent provisions.
4. **DeepMind, Three Layers.** Policy framework separating individual-agent security (upstream and downstream interventions, standards readiness), multi-agent risks (cascading failure, information-environment weaponisation), and empowering cyber defenders.
5. **EDPB Opinion 28/2024.** AI models trained on personal data are not automatically anonymous; sets out the case-by-case anonymity test, the three-step legitimate-interest test for development and deployment, reasonable expectations and mitigating measures, and consequences of unlawful development.
6. **Novation Strategy, DPDP in Practice.** Practitioner guide to the phased commencement (most duties and penalties from 13 May 2027), narrow lawful bases, Fiduciary responsibility for vendors, no-threshold breach reporting and high penalty ceilings.
7. **IAPP, 10 Tips.** Operating-model guidance for multi-jurisdictional compliance: scale assessment, trusted sources, tracking, risk measurement, jurisdiction/industry focus, strategy, audit, avoiding silos, privacy vs. cybersecurity, cultural humility.
8. **All Things Open / OSI, Open weights vs open source AI.** Explains the Open Source AI Definition (OSAID): "open weights" releases only the parameters, letting users fine-tune without seeing how the model was built; "open source AI" also releases training code and training data (or the fullest available description), so the model can be studied, rebuilt and modified. Why it matters for privacy and security teams: the EDPB's anonymity test for AI models (Opinion 28/2024) and any DPIA on a third-party model depend on knowing what data went in and how — an open-weight model gives you no more visibility into training data, memorisation risk or data-subject rights than a closed API does, so "open" on the label should not shortcut the lawfulness assessment. OSI reports G7 and UN engagement on a shared vocabulary, which is where regulatory carve-outs for "open" models are likely to be anchored.

9. **The Economist, Black October leader.** Argues that AI has, for now, tipped cyber-security toward attackers (PHP2Shell, eMudhra weak CA keys, European refrigeration attacks) because defenders lack access to frontier cyber-capable models that are gated by US government approval (Project Glasswing and its OpenAI counterpart), patches are reverse-engineered into exploits within hours, and automated exploitation punishes ordinary carelessness at scale. Prescribes a deliberate capability imbalance in defenders' favour, faster approval of programme members, resourcing for weak infrastructure operators (Texas Project Watershed 250) and, longer term, formal verification.
10. **NIST IR 8596, Cyber AI Profile (preliminary draft).** CSF 2.0 Community Profile organising AI cybersecurity around three Focus Areas — securing AI components, AI-enabled defence, thwarting AI-enabled attacks — with proposed priorities and informative references per Subcategory; companion to the SP 800-53 COSAiS overlays.
11. **Villasuso Lago, Export controls meet cybersecurity.** Argues that export-control compliance and cyber assurance now share one control surface at the identity-and-access layer: cyber-triggered access directives, the July 2026 agentic incidents read as reward hacking, distillation campaigns as a national-security vector, and the operating implications for organisations with foreign-national access to hosted frontier models.
12. **Leaders Advocates, UAE Cybercrime Law.** Explains the reach of FDL 34/2021 (everyone present in the UAE; extraterritorial where content targets the UAE or its residents), the Art. 43 defamation/insult offence where truth and sincerity are no defence, Art. 58 co-liability of entities and their managers, settlement options, the distinction from the PDPL, and Cassation 1829/2025 on the victim's complaint requirement.
13. **ABToken, Compliance and Cybersecurity Guide.** Maps the BCB Res. 519/520/521/2025 regime bringing Brazilian VASPs into the National Financial System: segregation, Travel Rule, KYT for self-custody wallets, AML/CFT, banking secrecy (CMN 5.280/2026), and a bank-grade cyber chapter (Res. 85/2021 extended; independent key-custody certification; MFA; pentests; endpoint monitoring; incident response).
