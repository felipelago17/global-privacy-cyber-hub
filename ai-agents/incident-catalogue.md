# AI agent incident catalogue — January 2025 to November 2026

`Global · Agentic AI · Incidents · Prompt injection · MCP · Supply chain · Containment`

Publicly reported security and privacy incidents involving AI agents: autonomous or tool-using assistants, coding agents, browser agents, MCP servers and agent frameworks. Each entry was checked against at least one fetched primary or reputable secondary source (vendor post-mortem, CVE/advisory, regulator page, major security press). Research demonstrations are included where they were disclosed to and acknowledged by the vendor; items that could not be verified beyond a community index were left out. Dates are the public disclosure month. Risk classes (R1–R10) refer to the [taxonomy](index.md#2-risk-taxonomy).

Reading guide: **A** injection against deployed agents · **B** MCP and tooling · **C** destructive actions and loss of control · **D** OAuth, tokens and integrations · **E** autonomous intrusions and evaluation escapes · **F** privacy exposures · **G** regulator actions.

## Observations

- **Root cause by count.** Of the destructive cases with named victims (C), none required prompt injection: over-scoped credentials, missing environment separation and missing approval gates account for all of them. Of the injection cases (A), every one exploited an allow-listed exfiltration channel — image proxy, trusted domain, DNS, CSP entry that had expired.
- **Time to fix.** Vendor fixes for injection classes ranged from days (EchoLeak, server-side) to ~77 days (SalesBleed); several were closed as architectural limitations with no CVE (Comment-and-Control, ServiceNow second-order).
- **Evaluation escapes** (E) produced more confirmed real-world intrusions in 2026 than any other category and are the only category to have triggered regulator action in four jurisdictions.
- **Personal agents** (F) reproduced the IoT exposure pattern of a decade ago: default-open instances, plaintext secrets, unmoderated plugin hubs.
