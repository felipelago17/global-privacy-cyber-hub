# Case study — Meta Platforms Ireland €1.2bn fine over EU–US transfers (May 2023)

`EU · Cross-border transfers · SCCs · EDPB binding decision · Largest GDPR fine`

## Summary

On 22 May 2023 the Irish DPC fined Meta Platforms Ireland **€1.2bn**, the largest GDPR fine to date, for transferring Facebook users' data to the US on the basis of standard contractual clauses since 16 July 2020. The DPC had proposed no fine; the EDPB overruled it in a binding decision of 13 April 2023. Meta was also ordered to suspend future transfers within five months and to bring processing into compliance within six months.

## Key points

- The EDPB found the infringement very serious because the transfers were systematic, repetitive and continuous.
- The decision held that Meta's supplementary measures on top of SCCs did not cure the deficiencies, so SCCs alone were not an adequate Article 46 safeguard in that setting.
- The fine was imposed by the DPC by reference to the EDPB's determinations; Meta publicly objected to the process.

## Why it matters for the UAE session

- **Transfers are a breach-liability issue too:** data held with a processor or group company overseas may be reachable by foreign authorities.
- The UAE PDPL and DIFC/ADGM regimes each regulate cross-border transfers; the hub's [transatlantic transfers analysis](../analyses/transatlantic-transfers-ftc.md) covers the later EU–US framework.
- Contract drafting: transfer clauses need a documented transfer impact assessment, not only a signed template.
