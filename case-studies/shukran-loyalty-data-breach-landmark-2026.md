# Case study — Shukran loyalty programme data breach, Landmark Group (November 2026)

`UAE · Retail · Loyalty data · Breach notification · Incident communication · Developing`

Status as of 12 Nov 2026. The incident was disclosed on 11 Nov 2026 and the investigation is ongoing; facts below come from press reports of company statements and may change.

## Summary

Landmark Group, the Dubai-based retail group, told members of its **Shukran** loyalty programme that an unauthorised party had accessed some personal information held on one of its systems. The company said names, contact details and dates of birth were involved, and that payment card details, passwords, points balances and wallets were not affected. Members who received an SMS about the incident were told to assume their records were among those involved, and were urged to watch for scam calls and messages. Landmark said it had informed statutory and law enforcement authorities and contained the incident while it investigates.

## Why it matters

- **Targeted notification and clear negatives.** The company said what was and was not affected and warned of follow-on scams, a pattern worth comparing with the Marriott and Meta cases.
- **Low-sensitivity fields still create risk.** Name, phone, email and date of birth together support phishing and impersonation.
- **Legal frame is uncertain.** The UAE PDPL's detailed breach mechanics depend on executive regulations whose issue status is disputed (see the [regimes note](../resources/frameworks/breach-liability-regimes-uae-difc-adgm-india.md)). The public reports describe a voluntary-style notice; they do not say which legal duty was relied on.
- **Questions for readers:** who decided that cardholder and password data were out of scope, and on what evidence? What was the basis for limiting alerts to affected members? How were processors and vendors covered?

## Framework mapping

PDPL security and breach duties (pending executive regulations); NIST CSF 2.0 Respond and Recover functions; ISO/IEC 27035 incident management. Comparators: GDPR Arts. 33–34, ADGM 72-hour notice.
