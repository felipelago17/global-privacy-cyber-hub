# Case study — Mata v. Avianca and ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2023–2024)

`United States · Generative AI · Fabricated citations · Professional responsibility · Verification controls`

## Summary

In June 2023 a federal judge in New York sanctioned two lawyers and their firm for filing a brief that cited court decisions invented by ChatGPT, and for standing by those citations after they were questioned. A year later the American Bar Association issued Formal Opinion 512 on lawyers' use of generative AI. Together they are the reference point for the rule that using an AI tool does not move the duty to verify. The lesson carries over to compliance, privacy and security work, where an invented regulation or a missing exception does the same damage as an invented case.

## Key facts

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Case | *Mata v. Avianca, Inc.*, 678 F. Supp. 3d 443 (S.D.N.Y. 2023), No. 22-cv-1461 (PKC) |
| Judge | P. Kevin Castel |
| Underlying claim | Personal-injury claim by a passenger against an airline; the airline moved to dismiss in January 2023 as time-barred under the Montreal Convention |
| What happened | The plaintiff's opposition cited decisions that did not exist. Opposing counsel could not find them. The lawyers continued to rely on them until late May 2023 |
| Hearing | 8 June 2023 |
| Order | 22 June 2023: finding of subjective bad faith; penalty of USD 5,000, with the firm jointly and severally liable; letters to be sent to the client and to the judges falsely named as authors of the invented opinions |
| ABA Formal Opinion 512 | Issued July 2024 by the Standing Committee on Ethics and Professional Responsibility; addresses generative AI tools |

## What went wrong

- **Research was delegated to a tool that generates text, not one that retrieves authority.** Nobody opened the decisions.
- **The error was defended, not corrected.** The court's criticism centred on what the lawyers did after the citations were challenged.
- **No second reader.** The filing lawyer signed work he had not checked.

## Why it matters for privacy, cyber and compliance programmes

- **Verification is a control, not a courtesy.** A policy, DPIA or regulatory memo that cites a non-existent provision is a defective record.
- **The sanction was small; the exposure was not.** The lasting cost was reputational, and the order required the lawyers to notify the people affected.
- **Confidentiality is the other half.** Opinion 512 is commonly summarised as covering competence, confidentiality, communication with clients, candour to the tribunal, supervision and fees. Entering client or personal data into a tool is a data-protection question as well as an ethics one.
- **Omissions count.** A fluent summary that drops an exception, a definition or a commencement date fails the same test.

## Controls to take from it

| Control | Practice |
|---|---|
| Ground first | Start from retrieved primary sources, not model recall |
| Open every authority | Read the provision or decision in the original and record that it was read |
| Match the proposition | Confirm the source supports the exact statement made |
| Carry a link or identifier | A citation that cannot be opened is treated as unverified |
| Adversarial pass | A second reviewer, or a separately instructed tool, looks only for errors |
| Flag uncertainty | Say when a source could not be found or sources conflict |
| Log and sign | Keep prompts and retrievals; a named person accepts responsibility |

## Caveats

- The details of the order were taken from the reported decision's headnote and from contemporaneous reporting. Opinion 512 was not re-read for this note; its scope is stated from general knowledge and should be checked against the ABA text before citation.
- Public trackers of later AI-citation cases exist but are not court databases; this hub does not rely on their counts.
