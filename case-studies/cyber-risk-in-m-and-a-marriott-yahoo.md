# Case study — Cyber risk in M&A: Marriott/Starwood and Verizon/Yahoo

`UK/EU · United States · Due diligence · Inherited breach · Price adjustment · GDPR`

## Summary

Two deals show what an undiscovered breach costs the acquirer. **Marriott** bought Starwood in 2016 without detecting an intrusion that began in 2014 and was found only in September 2018; the UK ICO fined Marriott £18.4m in October 2020. **Verizon** agreed in July 2016 to buy Yahoo's operating business for US$4.83bn; Yahoo then disclosed two breaches, and the price was cut by US$350m with the parties sharing breach liabilities.

## Marriott / Starwood

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Intrusion | An attacker installed a web shell on a Starwood system in 2014; undetected until September 2018 |
| Scale | About 339m guest records worldwide (7m relating to UK residents) |
| Penalty | ICO notice of intent £99m (July 2019); final fine £18.4m (30 Oct 2020), reduced for COVID-19 impact and mitigation steps; Marriott did not appeal and made no admission of liability |
| Scope of fine | Only the period from GDPR coming into force on 25 May 2018; the ICO acted as lead authority for the EU and the penalty was approved through the GDPR cooperation process |
| Finding | Failure to put in place appropriate technical or organisational measures |

**Lesson:** the acquirer inherits the target's security failures once it controls the systems; post-closing monitoring of acquired networks is part of "reasonable measures".

## Verizon / Yahoo

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Original price | US$4.83bn (July 2016) |
| Breaches | One compromising more than 500m accounts and another more than 1bn, disclosed after signing |
| Outcome | Price reduced by US$350m to about US$4.48bn; the breaches were not treated as a "business material adverse effect" |
| Liability split | Verizon bears half of non-SEC government investigation and third-party suit costs; Yahoo keeps the other half plus shareholder suits and SEC investigations |

**Lesson:** cyber diligence findings can be priced, ring-fenced by a specific indemnity or liability split, or treated as an MAE; decide this explicitly in the SPA.

## Practical diligence points (for panel discussion)

- Ask for incident history, regulator correspondence and penetration-test reports, and test their completeness.
- Add breach-specific representations, indemnities and an MAE position.
- Plan day-one integration: credentials, network segmentation, logging.
- Hiring and firing in deals: remove access promptly for departing staff.
