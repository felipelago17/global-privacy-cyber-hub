# Case study — Data-breach class actions and regulator settlements: Equifax, Capital One, Optus, Medibank

`United States · Australia · Class actions · Civil penalties · What "reasonable security" means in court`

## Summary

Four large breaches show how plaintiffs and regulators argue about "reasonable security measures". Equifax and Capital One are concluded, with money and mandated spending. Optus and Medibank are still before the Australian Federal Court and are the best live source of alleged-control lists.

## Equifax (breach 2017; settlement 2019)

- Settlement of up to **US$700m** with the CFPB, the FTC and 48 states plus DC and Puerto Rico: up to US$425m in consumer relief, a US$100m civil penalty and other relief.
- Roughly 147m people affected; the attack went undetected for more than six weeks.
- At least **US$1bn over five years** on security improvements.

## Capital One (breach 2019)

- **US$190m** class settlement covering about 98m US individuals; the settlement also released AWS.
- **US$80m** penalty to the OCC (with a parallel Federal Reserve settlement), because the bank allegedly failed to assess risks before moving IT operations to the cloud.
- The intruder was a former AWS engineer; the breach did not need insider knowledge, and involved a misconfigured web application firewall.

## Optus (breach 2022) and Medibank (breach 2022) — still live

- **OAIC v Optus:** civil penalty proceedings filed 8 August 2025; about 9.5m people; up to A$2.22m per contravention, and the OAIC alleges one contravention per person.
- **OAIC v Medibank:** civil penalty proceedings filed June 2024; alleged serious interference with the privacy of 9.7m people.
- **Class actions:** consumer actions against both (and a shareholder action against Medibank). On 2 October 2026 Justice Beach heard whether the Medibank class action and the OAIC case should be tried together; the class action alleges four core security failures, any of which it says could have prevented the breach.
- First Privacy Act civil penalty: **Australian Clinical Labs, A$5.8m** (October 2025).
- Allens maintains a table comparing the security measures alleged to be required in these cases; use it as a ready-made checklist.

## Why it matters

- **Reasonable measures is being defined by pleadings.** Allegations include basic controls (MFA, privileged access, logging, data deletion).
- **Regulator and plaintiff cases run in parallel**, and discovery in one feeds the other.
- **Cloud migration and misconfiguration** are board-level risks (Capital One).
- **UAE angle:** DIFC amended its law in July 2025 to give a private right of action in the DIFC Courts; the first claims will test the same arguments.
