## Summary

The alert starts from a scenario: threat actors use agentic AI to launch attacks that can spread across a corporate network in minutes, and the attack may expose most or all of an organisation's systems and data. The question is how the organisation should decide what to do. The answer is short. AI-enabled attacks have arrived and it is unclear that traditional incident response practice and legal frameworks will hold. Existing statutory, contractual and ethical breach-notification duties still apply, but working out what was affected and how to communicate about it is harder than before. The firm tells organisations to test whether their incident response is ready for a much larger "blast radius", more complex legal questions and business disruption becoming routine.

## What the alert says

1. **The premise is speed and reach.** Agentic attacks can move across a whole network in minutes (stated in the question).
2. **Obligations do not change.** Statutory, contractual and ethical notification duties continue to apply.
3. **Scoping and communications get harder.** Identifying affected information and systems, and deciding what to say and to whom, is more difficult when the attack touches most of the estate.
4. **Readiness is the recommendation.** Evaluate current incident response against a larger blast radius, harder legal questions and routine disruption.
5. **Further detail** is in the full post on the Data Decoded site, which I could not retrieve in full.

## Why it matters

- It is a plain statement from a major US firm that the standard breach playbook is not assumed to work against agentic attacks. That is useful in board and audit conversations.
- The notification point cuts both ways: the duties stay fixed while the time to scope an incident shrinks. Mapping obligations by jurisdiction, contract and sector in advance is the only way to meet short deadlines.
- "Which systems and data were affected" becomes the hardest early question when the attack's reach is wide. Asset and data inventories, logging retention and the ability to isolate segments quickly determine how fast it can be answered.
