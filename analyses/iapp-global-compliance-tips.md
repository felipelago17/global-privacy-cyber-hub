# "10 Tips for Global Compliance" — IAPP Privacy Bar Section Advisory Board (2024)

`Global · Privacy programme management · Multi-jurisdictional compliance`

## Source

- **Title:** 10 Tips for Global Compliance
- **Author:** IAPP Privacy Bar Section Advisory Board
- **Date:** 2024
- **Type:** Practitioner guidance
- **Link:** see [`resources/papers/links.md`](../resources/papers/links.md)

## Summary

Short, practical guidance from privacy practitioners on running a compliance programme across many jurisdictions. The value is less in any single tip than in the **operating model** they describe together: scope the problem, pick trustworthy sources, measure risk, prioritise, build strategy, audit, and avoid organisational and disciplinary silos.

## The ten tips (paraphrased)

| # | Tip | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Assess the scale of compliance** | Map where you have establishments, customers, employees, data and infrastructure; the footprint drives which laws apply |
| 2 | **Identify trusted sources** | Rely on regulators' own publications, reputable trackers and a small number of vetted advisers rather than ad-hoc news |
| 3 | **Track developments** | Maintain a horizon-scanning routine with owners and cadence; laws and guidance change monthly |
| 4 | **Measure risk** | Score jurisdictions and processing activities by enforcement likelihood, penalty exposure and reputational impact |
| 5 | **Focus by jurisdiction and industry** | Sector rules (health, finance, telecoms, children) often matter more than the general law |
| 6 | **Develop a strategy** | Decide between a global high-water-mark approach and jurisdiction-specific layering; document the choice |
| 7 | **Audit** | Test the programme periodically; evidence is what regulators and auditors will ask for |
| 8 | **Avoid legal silos** | Privacy, cybersecurity, AI governance, consumer protection, employment and competition law overlap; coordinate across them |
| 9 | **Differentiate privacy from cybersecurity** | Related but distinct disciplines with different owners, controls and regulators; do not let one absorb the other |
| 10 | **Practise cultural humility** | Local expectations of privacy, consent and authority differ; engage local counsel and stakeholders rather than exporting one model |

## Practical implications

- Tips 1, 4 and 6 together form the **programme design**; tips 2 and 3 form the **intelligence function**; tips 7–9 form the **assurance and governance** layer; tip 10 is the **change-management** lens.
- Use the list as a **maturity checklist**: score each tip 0–3 for your organisation and prioritise the lowest.
- Tip 8 is the one most relevant to this hub — the analyses here deliberately cross privacy, cybersecurity and AI governance.
- Tip 9 is a useful corrective in organisations where privacy sits inside the CISO function (or vice versa).

## Open questions / critique

- The tips are general by design; they do not resolve the hardest question — how to handle direct conflicts between jurisdictions (e.g. localisation vs. transfer obligations).
- No treatment of AI governance as a distinct discipline, which post-2024 would arguably be tip 11.
