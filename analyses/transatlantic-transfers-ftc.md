# US Supreme Court on FTC independence — implications for the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and UK–US Data Bridge (CMS)

`US · EU · UK · International transfers · DPF · Data Bridge`

## Source

- **Title:** CMS client briefing on the US Supreme Court ruling concerning presidential removal power over FTC commissioners
- **Author:** CMS (law firm)
- **Date:** 2026
- **Type:** Law-firm briefing
- **Link:** see [`resources/papers/links.md`](../resources/papers/links.md)

## Summary

The US Supreme Court's ruling expands the President's power to remove Federal Trade Commission commissioners, cutting back the independence the FTC has enjoyed since *Humphrey's Executor* (1935). The FTC is the primary enforcer of the commercial side of the **EU–US Data Privacy Framework (DPF)** and the **UK–US Data Bridge**. The EU adequacy decision for the DPF relied on the existence of independent oversight and effective enforcement; CMS flags that this ruling creates **new uncertainty** about whether those conditions still hold, and recommends that businesses reassess their transfer risk mitigation.

## Key findings

1. **What changed.** The Court held that statutory for-cause removal protections for FTC commissioners are unconstitutional (or substantially narrowed), bringing the agency closer to direct presidential control.
2. **Why it matters for transfers.** The European Commission's adequacy decision (July 2023) cites FTC enforcement of DPF commitments as part of the "essentially equivalent" protection. The Court of Justice in *Schrems II* struck down Privacy Shield partly on oversight and redress grounds; independence of enforcement bodies is a live criterion.
3. **Litigation risk.** Challenges to the DPF (e.g. *Latombe v Commission*) and any new NGO action will likely cite the ruling. The PCLOB staffing changes earlier in 2025–26 are a parallel concern on the national-security side.
4. **UK position.** The UK–US Data Bridge is an extension of the DPF; if the EU adequacy decision falls or is suspended, the UK would face pressure to follow, though its regime is procedurally separate.

## Practical implications

- **Inventory DPF-reliant transfers** now, distinguishing those with no fallback from those already covered by SCCs/IDTA.
- **Layer safeguards:** put SCCs (EU) / IDTA or Addendum (UK) in place *alongside* DPF certification for important flows, with Transfer Impact Assessments updated to reflect the ruling.
- **Supplementary measures:** encryption with EU/UK-held keys, pseudonymisation and data minimisation reduce exposure if adequacy is suspended.
- **Monitor** the Commission's periodic review of the DPF, EDPB statements, and CJEU docket for *Latombe* and related cases.
- **Cloud contracts:** check whether hyperscaler DPAs rely on DPF as the primary mechanism and what the automatic fallback is (see [gcp-dpia-summary.md](gcp-dpia-summary.md)).

## Open questions / critique

- The Commission may conclude that FTC independence was never load-bearing for adequacy, since enforcement capacity, not independence, is the DPF commitment; the briefing acknowledges the outcome is uncertain.
- No immediate suspension has occurred; the risk is prospective and litigation-driven.
