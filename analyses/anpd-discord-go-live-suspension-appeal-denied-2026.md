# Brazil ANPD: Discord's appeal against suspension of "Go Live" denied (Nov 2026)

`Brazil · ANPD · Digital ECA (Law 15,211/2025) · Children's online safety · Precautionary measures · Judicial reservation · Source: ANPD Board vote (unofficial English translation)`

Status as of 19 Nov 2026. This page summarises a 23-page PDF supplied by the author: an **unofficial English translation** of Vote No. 13/2026/GABDIR2/CD/ANPD in ANPD Oversight Case No. 00261.004804/2026-54. ## Outcome

The rapporteur, Interim Chair-Director Iagê Zendron Miola, signed his vote on 16 Nov 2026 to admit the appeal, deny suspensive effect, and deny the appeal on the merits, upholding Decisional Order No. 3/2026/SFI. The PDF also contains the votes of Directors Lorena Giuberti Coutinho (17 Nov) and Miriam Wimmer (18 Nov), each marked as following the rapporteur. The result is that the precautionary suspension of Discord's "Go Live" and functionally equivalent video-sharing features for users in Brazil stays in place. I could not tell from the PDF whether further directors voted, so I cannot confirm "unanimous".

## Background

- **Measure.** After opening an oversight proceeding on 7 Aug 2026 under the Child and Adolescent Digital Statute (Digital ECA, Law 15,211/2025) and Decree 12,880/2026, the ANPD's Oversight Superintendency (SFI) issued Decisional Order 3/2026 on 12 Aug. It suspended "Go Live" and similar real-time video transmission and screen-sharing features for users in Brazil until Discord shows reasonable risk-mitigation measures and gets the ANPD's prior authorisation to reactivate. One-to-one calls, voice, text and the rest of the service were kept. Discord implemented the suspension on 17 Aug.
- **Why.** An ANPD technical note found, on summary assessment, signs of structural insufficiency in the platform's safeguards, legal plausibility of administrative action and a risk of serious, hard-to-repair harm. The ANPD describes the risk as arising from combinations of features that create predictable paths to exposure, grooming, intimidation, violence, coercion and inducement to self-harm or suicide.
- **Later evidence.** A report from a São Paulo state public-security unit (received 31 Aug) was added; Discord was given access and five business days to respond (it did, on 15 Oct). The ANPD also asked about third-party "Activities" apps that might offer similar features, and on 26 Oct set out minimum technical requirements and maturity benchmarks, with a consolidated response due within 15 business days (extendable once).

## Discord's arguments and the Board's answers (as summarised in the vote)

- **Judicial reservation.** Discord said the measure equals the temporary-suspension sanction in Art. 35(III), which only a court may impose. The vote distinguishes a sanction, which punishes an established infringement at the end of a sanctioning proceeding, from a provisional, reversible precautionary measure aimed at one feature, not the application or the company. It says use of the word "suspend" does not change the legal nature and relies on earlier ANPD practice and court endorsement of its precautionary powers.
- **Competence and process.** The Superintendency, not the Board, could adopt the measure under the Oversight Regulation; the Board returned the file so Discord could comment on the new report, preserving adversarial process.
- **Gradation and the responsive approach.** Art. 34(2) of the Digital ECA does not require lighter steps first where the likelihood and seriousness of harm are high, particularly for children, whose protection has constitutional priority.
- **Proportionality.** The measure hits the feature layer that combines real-time audiovisual communication, low observability, potentially large audiences and users of unassured age. The vote says the harms, in cases of self-harm and suicide, are irreversible, while the company's harm, temporary unavailability of lawful uses, is reversible on demonstrating safeguards. Removal of accounts and servers after one episode did not show the structural risk was resolved.
- **Suspensive effect.** Denied: lifting the measure would restore the features whose safeguards were judged insufficient, and reversal depends on demonstrating safeguards, not on the appeal.
- **Discord's other points.** It argued non-retroactivity (facts from May 2025 to January 2026), flawed data in the report, and proactive conduct (it said it removed 10,121 servers on its own initiative against 99 at authorities' request) and safeguards since March 2026 such as age assurance and an expanded Family Center. The vote says these did not change the conclusions. The Board also declined to set general criteria in this appeal, saying general rules belong in the regulatory channel.

## Why a privacy and cyber hub should care

- **Regulator uses feature-level interim measures.** A data protection authority acting as digital child-safety regulator suspended a specific feature before any sanction, and the Board backed it. Platforms with Brazilian users, and companies assessing children's-safety exposure there, should expect this tool to be used.
- **Burden on the provider.** Reactivation requires the provider to demonstrate effective detection, escalation and response, plus age assurance; "isolated failure" arguments did not remove that burden.
