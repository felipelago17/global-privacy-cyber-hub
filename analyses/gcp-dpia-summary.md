# DPIA on Google Cloud Platform — Dutch government / Privacy Company (June 2026)

`EU · Netherlands · Cloud · GDPR · Public sector`

## Source

- **Title:** Data Protection Impact Assessment on Google Cloud Platform (Compute Engine, Cloud SQL (MySQL), Cloud Storage, BigQuery)
- **Commissioned by:** Dutch Ministry (central government procurement, SLM Rijk) — conducted by Privacy Company
- **Date:** June 2026
- **Type:** Government DPIA under GDPR Article 35
- **Link:** see [`resources/papers/links.md`](../resources/papers/links.md)

## Summary

A full DPIA of four core GCP services used by Dutch central government. The outcome is **11 low data-protection risks** and no high risks remaining, subject to the configuration choices and contractual measures the report describes. The report is a reference point for any organisation assessing IaaS/PaaS usage under GDPR, and a useful template for cloud compliance checklists.

## Key findings

1. **Five categories of personal data** are distinguished and assessed separately:
   - *Content data* (what customers upload or process in the services)
   - *Account data* (identity of admins/users)
   - *Diagnostic / telemetry data* (logs, metrics, usage events generated by the platform)
   - *Support data* (tickets and related exchanges)
   - *Website data* (console, documentation and marketing surfaces)
2. **Roles:** Google acts as **processor** for Content data and for most Diagnostic data; the report tests where Google positions itself as **controller** (e.g. certain account, support and website data) and whether that is justified.
3. **Data residency and transfers:** data-location controls, regionalisation options and the position on transfers to the US are analysed, including reliance on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and the fallback to SCCs and supplementary measures.
4. **Encryption and confidential computing:** customer-managed encryption keys and confidential-computing options materially reduce the residual risk for Content data; the report treats them as recommended mitigations, not defaults.
5. **Retention:** retention periods for telemetry and logs are reviewed; residual risk depends on admins configuring log retention and limiting telemetry where possible.
6. **Result:** 11 low risks across the four services, each paired with a mitigating measure for Google and/or the customer organisation.

## Practical implications

- Use the **five-category data model** as the backbone of any cloud DPIA — it forces clarity on who controls what and where the gaps are.
- Treat the **customer-side measures** (key management, logging configuration, region selection, admin access governance) as mandatory to reach the "low risk" outcome; the DPIA's conclusion is conditional on them.
- The **processor/controller analysis** is reusable for other hyperscalers: challenge any "controller for diagnostic data" position.
- For **non-EU organisations** (e.g. GCC, UK, India), the structure translates directly; replace the EU transfer analysis with the relevant local transfer regime (UAE PDPL, DIFC/ADGM, UK IDTA, DPDP Rules).

## Open questions / critique

- The outcome is tied to a specific contractual baseline negotiated for the Dutch government; organisations without that leverage may not reach the same residual-risk level.
- The DPIA post-dates the EU–US DPF but pre-dates the full consequences of the 2026 US Supreme Court decision on FTC independence (see [transatlantic-transfers-ftc.md](transatlantic-transfers-ftc.md)); the transfer section may need re-reading in that light.
