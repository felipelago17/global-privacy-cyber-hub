## What the page says

- **The amendment.** Oman significantly amended its Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, enacted 2022) through Royal Decree No. 68/2026, described as the most substantial update since 2022. The summary gives the effective date as 7 Sept 2026.
- **Territorial scope.** Expanded, and the article suggests it may bring more multinational processing within scope.
- **Lawful grounds.** New circumstances allow processing without explicit consent.
- **Automated processing.** A new framework for automated processing and automated decision-making, described as a safeguard for AI-driven decisions affecting individuals.
- **Employment and security.** More flexibility for certain employment-related and security-related processing.
- **GDPR comparison.** Several concepts move closer to international standards, including some familiar from the GDPR. Oman keeps distinctive features on sensitive data, government oversight and cross-border transfers.

## Corroborating context (search excerpts only; not read in full)

- Other law-firm notes call the change the first significant update to the PDPL since 2022 (CMS) or note a related Tamimi piece dated 15 Oct 2026.
- **Base law.** The PDPL was issued under Royal Decree 6/2022 and came into force on 13 Feb 2023 (CMS guide; PwC).
- **Executive regulations.** PwC says they came into force on 5 Feb 2024, with a grace period to 5 Feb 2025, and Clyde & Co says the ministry issued them on 28 Jan 2024. CMS cites them as Ministerial Decision 34/2024 and says the law became fully enforceable on 5 Feb 2026 after the transition period. One CMS expert-guide excerpt says that as of 2026 the executive regulations are "expected" to address transfers, impact assessments and DPOs, which sits uneasily with those other sources, and a Trowers excerpt refers to implementing regulations under Ministerial Decision 50/2026. I have not reconciled these and the discrepancy needs checking against the Official Gazette or the ministry.
- **Regulator.** PwC names the Ministry of Transport, Communications and Information Technology as the competent authority.
- **Related law.** A CMS excerpt mentions a Cyber Crime Law under Royal Decree 61/2026 creating offences for misuse of personal data obtained through IT systems. Not verified.

## Why a privacy and cyber hub should care

- **Extraterritorial reach.** If scope now catches processing outside Oman that touches people in Oman, regional and global operating models need a fresh look.
- **Consent reliance.** New non-consent grounds may change how consent-based programmes are designed, but the grounds are not specified in what I read.
- **Automated decisions.** The new framework links to the AI governance material in this hub, including the [EDPB coordinated enforcement note on automated recruitment under Art. 22](edpb-cef-2027-automated-recruitment-art22.md).
