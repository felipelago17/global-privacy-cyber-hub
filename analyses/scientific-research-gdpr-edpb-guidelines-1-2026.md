## Thesis

The GDPR does not define scientific research, so the boundary is unclear. The EDPB's draft guidelines help with six indicative factors. Commercial interest does not by itself disqualify research, but the activity must be genuinely scientific. AI model training may qualify, but not automatically, and failing to qualify does not make AI development unlawful because other legal bases may apply.

## Main points (as summarised)

- **Recital 159** supports a broad reading, including privately funded, technological development and applied research.
- **Article 5.** Research status brings presumed compatibility of further processing and longer retention, subject to Art. 89 safeguards (not discussed in detail).
- **Article 17.** A conditional erasure exception where erasure would make research impossible or seriously impair its objectives.
- **Article 9.** Special-category data still needs a separate Art. 9 condition.
- **EDPB Guidelines 1/2026** (adopted 15 Apr 2026 as the public-consultation version; consultation closed 25 June 2026, so not final). The six factors: methodical and systematic approach; ethical standards; verifiability and transparency; autonomy and independence; research objectives; scientific contribution or novelty. Meeting all six supports a presumption of research status, but missing one does not automatically exclude an activity, and the controller must justify its position. EDPB's press page and Hogan Lovells date the publication 16 Apr 2026.
- **Case law.** KHO:2013:181 (Finnish Supreme Administrative Court): pharmaceutical-company funding did not by itself prevent a study being scientific research, with emphasis on autonomy and publicity; the court upheld refusal of register access because the study's primary scientific aims were not sufficiently established. It concerned pre-GDPR Finnish rules on access to confidential records, not the GDPR definition.

## AI model training

- Training may qualify if it tests a new method with ethical safeguards, independent judgement and verifiable results. Routine product optimisation for commercial performance is unlikely to qualify.
- The EDPB example reportedly has a generative-AI startup and a university researching model bias with ethical review and peer-reviewed publication, which qualifies even if the startup may commercialise the results.
- Trade secrets do not automatically disqualify, but confidentiality must be balanced against verifiability.
- If training does not qualify, legitimate interests under Art. 6(1)(f) may still apply, per CNIL's Jan 2026 recommendations (as the article reports), which requires a specific interest, necessity and balancing. Art. 9 still applies to special-category data.

## What the article leaves out

The article does not discuss the Digital Omnibus, EDPS guidance, Art. 14(5), Art. 9(2)(j) specifically, or the Finnish Data Protection Act's research provisions. From search excerpts, the reform context matters:
- The Commission's Digital Omnibus proposal (COM(2025) 837 final, 19 Nov 2025, per a Tampere University consultation response) would add an Art. 3(1)(b) GDPR definition of scientific research. One consultation response (The Guild) quotes it as covering research that can also support innovation, such as technological development.
- The EDPB-EDPS Joint Opinion 2/2026 (10 Feb 2026) comments on the Omnibus, including the definition, a clarification that Art. 6(4) need not be applied, and a limited derogation to the duty to inform.
- Several consultation respondents asked how the guidelines relate to the Omnibus definition, and Tech Policy Press (Sept 2026) notes a possible tension between the AI-in-science policy and a GDPR definition that could exclude core AI development, training or testing.
- Respondents' views (including whether a six-factor test could become a de facto definition) are positions, not findings.

## Why a privacy and cyber hub should care

- **AI development and lawful basis.** Whether training counts as research changes the available compatibility and retention rules, but the guidance does not give a safe harbour, so organisations still need a documented lawful basis and Art. 9 condition.
- **Documentation.** The controller must justify research status, which needs governance records: ethics review, independence, publication plans.
- **Shifting rules.** The guidelines are draft and a statutory definition is proposed, so projects relying on the research exemption should watch both.
