## Summary

A practitioner guide to how India's DPDP regime operates now that the Rules have been notified. The headline is a **phased commencement**: most operational duties and the penalty regime become effective from **13 May 2027**, giving organisations a defined runway. Compared with GDPR, the regime is simpler in structure but **stricter** in several respects — narrower lawful bases, no materiality threshold for breach reporting, and high penalty ceilings.

## Key findings

| Topic | Position under DPDP Act + Rules |
|---|---|
| **Commencement** | Phased. Institutional provisions (Data Protection Board) first; most Data Fiduciary obligations and penalties from 13 May 2027 |
| **Lawful bases** | Only **consent** or the enumerated **"legitimate uses"** (e.g. voluntary provision for a specified purpose, employment, state functions, medical emergencies). **No general legitimate-interest basis** equivalent to GDPR Article 6(1)(f) |
| **Consent standard** | Free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, with clear affirmative action; notice must be in plain language and available in the scheduled Indian languages |
| **Roles** | **Data Fiduciary** (≈ controller) and **Data Processor**. The Fiduciary remains fully responsible for processing by its processors/vendors |
| **Breach notification** | **Every** personal data breach must be reported to the Data Protection Board **and** affected Data Principals — there is **no materiality or risk threshold**. Rules prescribe content and timelines |
| **Significant Data Fiduciaries** | Additional duties (DPO in India, independent audits, DPIAs, algorithmic due diligence, possible localisation conditions) for entities so notified |
| **Children** | Verifiable parental consent; restrictions on tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising to children |
| **Cross-border transfers** | Permitted by default except to countries/territories the government restricts by notification (a blacklist rather than adequacy model) |
| **Penalties** | High ceilings (up to INR 250 crore per instance for security-safeguard failures); graded for other breaches |
| **Data Principal rights** | Access, correction, erasure, grievance redressal, nomination; Fiduciaries must publish a grievance mechanism and respond within prescribed timelines |

## Practical implications

- **Re-paper your lawful basis.** Processing currently run on legitimate interest in the EU/UK has **no direct equivalent**; it must be mapped to consent or a listed legitimate use, or stopped.
- **Breach playbook:** because every breach is reportable, incident classification must distinguish "breach" from "security event" rigorously and early; notification templates should be pre-approved.
- **Vendor management:** the Fiduciary cannot shift responsibility; contracts need audit rights, breach-notification SLAs shorter than the statutory timelines, and sub-processor controls.
- **Consent architecture:** multilingual notices and consent withdrawal that is as easy as giving consent will require UX changes for India-facing services.
- **Use the runway.** The May 2027 date is the deadline for operational readiness, not the date to start.

## Open questions / critique

- Guidance from the Data Protection Board on breach-reporting practicalities and on what counts as a "legitimate use" in borderline cases is still thin.
- The blacklist approach to transfers is flexible but creates unpredictability; the first notified restrictions will be a key signal.
