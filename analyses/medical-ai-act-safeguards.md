# "High-risk without safeguards?" — EU AI Act and AI-enabled medical devices (BMJ Digital Health)

`EU · Healthcare · EU AI Act · MDR/IVDR · Medical AI`

## Source

- **Title:** High-risk without safeguards? The Health Innovation Package and AI-enabled medical devices under the EU AI Act
- **Venue:** BMJ Digital Health
- **Date:** 2026
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed commentary / analysis
- **Link:** see [`resources/papers/links.md`](../resources/papers/links.md)

## Summary

The article critiques the European Commission's **Health Innovation Package** proposals as they affect AI-enabled medical devices. Under the current AI Act, such devices are high-risk via **Annex I, Section A** (harmonised-legislation route) and attract the full set of high-risk obligations. The proposals would keep the "high-risk" label but **move AI-enabled medical devices to Annex I, Section B**, whose products are exempt from most of the AI Act's substantive requirements. The authors argue this shifts the centre of gravity back to the MDR/IVDR without equivalent AI-specific protections.

## Key findings

1. **Classification unchanged, obligations removed.** Devices would still be listed as high-risk, but Section B status means the AI Act's Chapter III, Section 2 requirements would largely not apply.
2. **Safeguards at stake** (the article identifies these as the main losses):
   - Data and data-governance requirements (Article 10) — training/validation/test data quality, bias examination
   - Risk-management system (Article 9) — lifecycle, AI-specific risk identification
   - Human-oversight design requirements (Article 14)
   - Post-market monitoring specific to AI behaviour and drift (Article 72)
   - Transparency and instructions for deployers (Article 13), logging (Article 12), accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity (Article 15)
3. **MDR/IVDR are not a substitute.** The medical-device regulations predate modern ML, treat software largely as static, and lack explicit provisions on training data, bias, continuous learning or algorithmic oversight. Notified bodies' AI expertise is uneven.
4. **Rationale and counter-rationale.** The Commission frames the change as reducing duplication and supporting innovation; the authors respond that the overlap could be handled by **integration** (e.g. recognising AI Act conformity within MDR assessment) rather than **removal**.

## Practical implications

- **Manufacturers:** do not plan compliance roadmaps on the assumption that Article 9–15 obligations will fall away; the proposal is contested and may change in the legislative process. Building to the full high-risk standard remains the defensible position.
- **Hospitals and deployers:** deployer duties (Article 26) may also shrink if devices move to Section B; procurement and clinical-governance teams should set contractual requirements that reproduce the AI Act safeguards regardless.
- **Regulators / notified bodies:** the article is an argument for building AI assessment capacity inside the MDR/IVDR system if the shift proceeds.
- **Comparative note:** jurisdictions drafting AI-in-health rules (UK MHRA, UAE DoH/MOHAP, Saudi SFDA) can read this as a case study in how framework interaction can silently remove protections.

## Open questions / critique

- The article is written against a **proposal**; the final text may restore some safeguards or add AI-specific content to MDR/IVDR.
- The authors do not quantify the compliance cost the Commission is trying to remove, which weakens the rebuttal to the innovation argument.
