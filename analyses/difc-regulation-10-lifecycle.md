# DIFC Regulation 10 — from enactment (2023) to the 2026 consultation

`UAE · DIFC · AI governance · Autonomous systems · Certification · Regulatory sandbox`

## Source

- **Instrument:** Regulation 10 of the DIFC Data Protection Regulations, "Processing Personal Data Through Autonomous and Semi-Autonomous Systems", made under DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020
- **Type:** Reading note assembled from the DIFC's own announcement and law-firm commentary. The DIFC website could not be opened when this note was prepared, so the Regulation's text was **not** read directly
- **Links:** see Sources

## Summary

Regulation 10 is the UAE's only binding, AI-specific data-protection rule. It attaches duties to the people who deploy or operate autonomous and semi-autonomous systems that process personal data, including generative AI. It matters for the distinction between using a model and training one: commentary on the Regulation says it applies when personal data is processed for use in, or to facilitate the learning processes of, an AI system. In 2026 the DIFC consulted on tightening it.

## Key points

### Who is responsible
- Duties fall on **deployers** (broadly comparable to controllers) and **operators** (technical providers acting for a deployer). The deployer is generally accountable for compliance.

### What is required
- Systems must be designed to be ethical, fair, transparent, secure and accountable.
- Before high-risk processing, privacy risks must be assessed, documented and mitigated.
- Individuals must be told that an autonomous system is in use and given enough information to evaluate the risks, with the ability to object or withdraw consent.
- High-risk processing brings further duties, including appointment of an **Autonomous Systems Officer** and certification requirements.
- Misleading notices or misstatements about certification can lead to investigation and enforcement.

### What the 2026 consultations propose
- Stronger safety-by-design requirements, a clearer and wider role for the Autonomous Systems Officer, and clarified certification obligations (Consultation Paper No. 3).
- An AI Adequacy Protocol that would score systems from 1 to 3 on four factors (data volume, decision impact, autonomy, data sensitivity) and sort them into Standard, Enhanced and Critical tiers, with an appeal route.

### Sandbox route
- A **Regulation 10 Accelerator** is described as a sandbox for testing systems against privacy-by-design principles and the Regulation's requirements. One report says testing periods of 6, 12 or 18 months are planned.

## Implications for compliance programmes

| Area | Action |
|---|---|
| Inventory | Record for each system whether personal data is used at inference only or also to enable learning; the second is squarely within the Regulation |
| Roles | Decide and document who is deployer and who is operator for each system; do not rely on contract labels |
| High-risk processing | Check whether an Autonomous Systems Officer and certification are required before go-live |
| Notices | Tie the notice to the actual technology and its privacy impact |
| Horizon | Track the outcome of Consultation Paper No. 3 and the Adequacy Protocol; budget for re-assessment |
| AI agents | Read with the hub's [regulatory map for AI agents](../ai-agents/regulatory-map.md) |
