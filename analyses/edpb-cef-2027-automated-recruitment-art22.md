# EDPB 2027 coordinated enforcement on automated decisions in recruitment, and the Article 22 case law behind it

`GDPR · Art. 22 · Art. 15(1)(h) · Recruitment · AI screening · CEF 2027 · Status: reported, not confirmed on EDPB site`

Status as of 12 Nov 2026.

## The announcement (reported)

A privacy-law academic's post of 12 Nov 2026 reports that the European Data Protection Board has selected **automated decision-making in recruitment** for its sixth Coordinated Enforcement Framework (CEF) action, to launch in 2027. As described in the post, the action will look at automated candidate screening, including AI-driven tools, the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions (Art. 22 GDPR) and the related right of access; data protection authorities join voluntarily.

**What I could and could not confirm.** The EDPB's Work Programme 2026–2027 (adopted 11 Feb 2026) provides for a sixth coordinated action in 2027 on a topic to be determined after the 2026 action is complete, which fits the report. I did not find the EDPB's own announcement of the 2027 topic, so treat the topic as reported until it appears on the EDPB site. For context, the 2026 action (the fifth) covers transparency and information obligations under Arts. 12–14 GDPR, selected in October 2025; participation is voluntary and national results are aggregated by the EDPB.

## Case law the action is likely to draw on

| Case | Status | Point |
|---|---|---|
| **C-203/22, CK v Dun & Bradstreet Austria** | CJEU judgment, 27 Feb 2025 | Art. 15(1)(h) "meaningful information about the logic involved" requires an explanation of the procedure and principles actually applied; handing over a complex formula or algorithm is not enough. It must let the person understand how their data were used and challenge the decision. Trade secrets do not automatically block disclosure; the competing interests are balanced, with review by the court or authority where needed |
| **C-568/25, VKI v Universal Versand** | Advocate General Medina's non-binding opinion, 15 Oct 2026; judgment pending | Concerns an automated credit check that limited payment options. Per MLex, the AG said a retailer relying on the contract-necessity exemption must show that automation is necessary and that no less intrusive alternative exists, with time and cost savings potentially relevant. The CJEU's answer is expected next year |

## Practical questions for recruitment tools

- **Solely automated?** When do screening scores or rankings effectively decide who progresses, and does the recruiter's involvement amount to a meaningful assessment of the application rather than a rubber stamp?
- **Significant effect:** whether rejection at screening stage reaches the Art. 22(1) threshold is one of the open questions the Universal Versand opinion touches, even though that case concerns payment options.
- **Explanation:** how can employers give candidates explanations specific and intelligible enough to challenge a decision, consistent with *Dun & Bradstreet*?
- **Access and evidence:** keep records of the logic, the data used, human review steps and how candidates can contest outcomes.
- **Overlap:** EU AI Act obligations for high-risk employment systems apply alongside the GDPR; see the [medical AI note](medical-ai-act-safeguards.md) for how the high-risk framework is being debated.

## Author's view (attributed)

The post argues that some data protection authorities, including Poland's UODO, have been quiet on Article 22 enforcement since the GDPR became applicable, and calls for more sustained engagement and guidance. That is one commentator's opinion, not a finding.

The post also mentions a newly published European Commission Digital Fairness Act proposal; I have not verified it and it is not covered here.
