# Luxembourg NIS2: ILR sets annual security-measures reporting, first deadline 15 March 2027

`NIS2 · Luxembourg · ILR · Essential entities · Art. 21 · Reporting deadline 15 Mar 2027`

Status as of 13 Nov 2026.

## What changed

According to a law-firm note published 12 Nov 2026 (Elvinger Hoss Prum), the Luxembourg regulator ILR adopted **Regulation N26/10 dated 26 October 2026**. It requires **essential entities, in all NIS2 sectors**, to file an annual submission with the ILR. The first deadline is **15 March 2027**.

The submission has three parts:

| Element | What the entity provides |
|---|---|
| Security measures | Description of the cybersecurity measures in place |
| Risk scenarios | Analysis of the key cyber-risk scenarios |
| Action plan | Multi-year plan to address the gaps |

The note also says the ILR may require certain categories of entities to submit **lists of their suppliers and service-provider dependencies**. Earlier ILR regulations (N22/8 for electronic communications and N22/7 for operators of essential services under NIS1) already required notifications in 2026, according to the same note.

## Context from other public sources

- Luxembourg transposed NIS2 by the **law of 5 May 2026**, in force since 10 May 2026 (Deloitte Luxembourg; Cullen International).
- Entities in scope had to **self-register with the ILR by 10 July 2026**; the ILR estimated 1,500 to 2,000 entities could be covered (Government of Luxembourg release of 6 July 2026; Paperjam).
- The ILR treats management-body accountability (NIS2 Art. 20) and the Art. 21(2) risk-management measures as the centre of supervision (ILR guidance of 17 Feb 2026 as described by a consultancy; secondary source).
- A consultancy article says the ILR published templates on 3 Aug 2026 that serve as an evidence baseline. I could not open the ILR template itself, so treat this as unverified.

## Why it matters

1. **Reporting becomes a recurring evidence exercise.** The filing is not an incident notification. It asks the entity to describe measures, name its main scenarios and commit to a roadmap. Whatever is filed is a statement to the regulator that can later be compared with the facts after an incident.
2. **Board-level sign-off.** Because Art. 20 makes management bodies approve and oversee the measures, the filing is a natural point for formal board approval and a documented challenge.
3. **Supply-chain visibility.** The possible supplier and dependency lists link directly to NIS2 Art. 21(2)(d) (supply-chain security). Entities with fragmented vendor inventories will find this the hardest element.
4. **Scenarios beyond personal data.** Risk scenarios for an essential entity will usually include operational technology, process data and proprietary information. See [Cybersecurity of sensitive non-personal data](sensitive-non-personal-data-security.md) for how NIS2 sits alongside trade-secret and other regimes.
5. **Cross-border groups.** Groups with entities in several Member States should expect different reporting formats and deadlines under each national NIS2 law, so a single group-level narrative will need local adaptation.

## Practical questions

- Is each Luxembourg entity classified as essential or important, and was self-registration completed by 10 July 2026?
- Who owns the three deliverables, and when will the board review them before 15 March 2027?
- Does the scenario analysis match the entity's actual incident history and its business-continuity tests?
- Can the entity produce a current list of critical suppliers, with the services each one supports?
- Do the multi-year plan's milestones have budget and named owners, since the regulator may check progress in later years?

## What this page does not establish

- The source note is the only source I read for Regulation N26/10. I did not find the regulation text, the ILR's own announcement or the ILR's filing template, so the details above (scope, content of the filing, deadline) rest on that note.
- The note does not say which categories of entities will have to submit supplier lists, what format the filing takes, what happens on late or inadequate filing, or whether **important** entities have a separate obligation.
- Penalties and supervisory powers under the Luxembourg law are not covered here; check the law of 5 May 2026 directly.
- Financial-sector entities are supervised by the CSSF, not the ILR, so this regulation should not be assumed to apply to them.
