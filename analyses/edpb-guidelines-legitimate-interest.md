# EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 on legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) — Version 2.0

`GDPR · Legal bases · Legitimate interest · EDPB · Version 2.0, 10 Nov 2026`

Status as of 12 Nov 2026. **Version 2.0 was adopted by the EDPB on 10 November 2026, after public consultation.** It replaces Version 1.0, which was adopted for public consultation on 8 October 2024. The version history in the PDF does not say what changed between the two, and I read only the first part of the document (about 100,000 of 161,843 characters), so the comparison below is limited to what the final text contains and what I know of the consultation draft.

## What Version 2.0 says (as read)

- Article 6(1)(f) is one of six legal bases. It is not a last resort, a default, or a way around stricter bases.
- Three cumulative conditions: (1) the controller or a third party pursues a legitimate interest; (2) the processing is necessary for it, meaning it cannot reasonably be achieved just as effectively by less restrictive means; (3) data subjects' interests and fundamental rights do not override it. The controller should assess and **document** this before processing starts.
- **Structure:** introduction; assessment steps (interest, necessity, balancing); relationship with data subject rights (transparency, access, objection, erasure, automated decisions, rectification, restriction); contextual application.
- **Contexts covered:** children (a dedicated section; children need specific protection, especially for marketing, profiling or services aimed at them); employees (assessment differs and employees may not share the controller's assumed interests); public authorities; fraud prevention; direct marketing (four subsections); group administration; network and information security; transmission to authorities.
- **Six worked examples**, including promotional emails for a product whose marketing is prohibited by law (interest not legitimate), neighbourhood-watch video surveillance (interest too vague), a newspaper database of former subscribers (interest hypothetical), and personalised social-network advertising and flyers using publicly posted photos (reasonable expectations not met).
- **AI:** no dedicated section. The text cites EDPB Opinion 28/2024 on AI models and a CJEU ruling (C-484/24) on unlawfully obtained data.
- Web scraping did not appear in the portion I read.

## Version 1.0 (consultation) points, for comparison

- Public consultation ran to 20 November 2024. The draft already set out the three conditions, the "not a last resort" statement, the requirement that an interest be lawful, clearly articulated and real and present, and the link to data subject rights.
- I could not verify which of the sections above are new in Version 2.0.

## Why it matters for breach, security and AI work

- **Fraud and security processing** (monitoring, logging, threat intelligence) often relies on legitimate interest; the documented assessment is evidence of accountability.
- **AI development and deployment:** read with the [EDPB Opinion 28/2024 on AI models](edpb-ai-models.md), which uses the same three-step test.
- **Transparency and expectations:** a processing activity can be technically lawful yet fail the balancing test if it would surprise the people affected. Retain the assessment, the safeguards considered and the reasons for the outcome.
- **Subsequent EDPB guidance builds on it:** later guidelines (for example on blockchain, adopted in final form on 7 July 2026, and on scientific research) cross-refer to Guidelines 1/2024.
