# EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 on legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) — final version

`GDPR · Legal bases · Legitimate interest · EDPB · Status: final version reported, check text`

Status as of 12 Nov 2026. A professional post dated 12 Nov 2026 reports that the European Data Protection Board has adopted the **final version** of its guidelines on processing based on legitimate interest, with the point that the assessment must reflect how processing works in practice, including people's reasonable expectations, safeguards and the ability to exercise rights. When this note was written, the EDPB page for the guidelines still showed **Version 1.0 (public consultation)** with a publication date of 9 October 2024, and I could not find a final adoption date, a Version 2.0 file or a change log. Treat the section below on content as the consultation text and compare it with the final PDF before relying on any detail.

## What the consultation version says (Version 1.0, adopted 8 Oct 2024)

- Article 6(1)(f) is not a "last resort" legal basis for rare or unexpected situations, and it should not be chosen automatically because it seems less constraining than other bases.
- Three cumulative conditions must be met, and controllers should assess and **document** them: (1) a legitimate interest of the controller or a third party; (2) the processing is necessary for that interest, with no equally effective, less intrusive alternative; (3) the interests, rights and freedoms of data subjects do not override the interest pursued.
- A legitimate interest must be lawful, clearly articulated, and real and present rather than speculative.
- The balancing test looks at the nature and source of the interest, the impact on data subjects, their reasonable expectations, and safeguards that reduce the impact.
- The guidelines also cover how Article 6(1)(f) interacts with data subject rights, including objection.
- Public consultation closed on 20 November 2024 and drew submissions from business associations, NGOs, academics and companies.

## Why it matters for breach, security and AI work

- **Fraud and security processing** (monitoring, logging, threat intelligence) often relies on legitimate interest; the documented assessment is evidence of accountability.
- **AI development and deployment:** read with the [EDPB Opinion 28/2024 on AI models](edpb-ai-models.md), which uses the same three-step test.
- **Transparency and expectations:** a processing activity can be technically lawful yet fail the balancing test if it would surprise the people affected. Retain the assessment, the safeguards considered and the reasons for the outcome.
- **Subsequent EDPB guidance builds on it:** later guidelines (for example on blockchain, adopted in final form on 7 July 2026, and on scientific research) cross-refer to Guidelines 1/2024.

## What to check in the final text

Confirm adoption date and version number; whether the examples and sector sections changed; any changes on children, direct marketing, employee monitoring and AI; and whether the summary document was updated.
