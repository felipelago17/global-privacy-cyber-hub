## The source

PYMNTS ("New York Creates AI Enforcement Unit as States Step Up Oversight"), 20 Nov 2026: <https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/new-york-creates-ai-enforcement-unit-as-states-step-up-oversight/>. The announcement was made on Tuesday 17 Nov 2026, per PYMNTS (which notes it came a day after California's suit) and CDO Magazine.

## What was announced

- **The unit.** The AI Safety, Standards, Enforcement, and Transparency (ASSET) Unit, created by New York Attorney General Letitia James. A German-language report says the AG's office calls it the first state-level unit in the United States dedicated solely to enforcing AI laws, which is the office's own claim.
- **Place and leadership.** Within the Economic Justice Division, working closely with the Bureau of Internet and Technology, led by Cristy Phillips, previously special counsel in the division. The article gives no staffing numbers.
- **Why it matters, per PYMNTS.** States are setting their own rules without a comprehensive federal AI framework, and attorneys general are testing how far existing laws reach.
- **Mandate.** Enforce the RAISE Act (New York's frontier-AI safety law) when it takes effect, help investigators across the office assess AI-related risks in privacy, cybersecurity, digital scams and online safety, and develop policy proposals and technical expertise for other divisions.
- **Targets.** Model developers, chatbot operators and other businesses whose AI use may violate state law.
- **Powers.** Civil actions to protect privacy, stop unfair, deceptive or abusive practices, and hold hackers and fraudsters accountable. James also cited the federal Computer Fraud and Abuse Act.

## Laws the unit will enforce (as listed)

| Law | What it does |
|---|---|
| RAISE Act | Requires covered frontier model developers to publish safety measures and promptly report qualifying security and safety incidents. Effective 1 Jan 2027 per CDO Magazine and a local-station report; the name is given in press as the Responsible AI Safety Education Act. The article gives no penalty amounts. |
| General Business Law, Art. 47 | Chatbots must make reasonable efforts to detect and respond to suicidal ideation and tell users they are interacting with AI |
| S.9051B / A.10379C | Prohibits certain unsafe chatbot features for children; effective June (a regional report says June 2027) |
| SHIELD Act | Reasonable data security safeguards |
| Synthetic Performer Disclosure Act | Disclosure when AI replaces a human performer in advertising |
| Penal Law § 245.15 | Criminalises intentionally disseminating intimate images intended to harm, including AI-generated or altered ones |

EconoTimes also describes a separate state office opening registration for large frontier AI developers ahead of a 1 Jan deadline and overseeing incident reporting; I have not confirmed which office or what the deadline covers.

## Wider state context (per PYMNTS)

- **California.** A day before the announcement, Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI, alleging it failed to secure and monitor testing of advanced models, creating a public nuisance and violating the Unfair Competition Law after an AI agent gained unauthorised access to Hugging Face systems. The complaint also alleges failure to monitor model activity adequately and to act on signs of misbehaviour. California seeks a court order, civil penalties and other relief. PYMNTS says Politico reports legal experts see it as a possible model for other attorneys general, since their authority to protect the public can give them a path to court that private plaintiffs may struggle to establish, and calls enforcement against developers "bicoastal"; OpenAI reportedly told Politico it felt singled out over a problem shared by major labs. These are allegations and one side's response.
- **New York's earlier steps.** James's office has solicited whistleblower complaints from AI industry employees and warned about AI-generated celebrity investment scams.

## Why a privacy and cyber hub should care

- **State enforcement fills the federal gap.** AI-specific enforcement is arriving through state attorneys general using existing privacy, consumer-protection and data-security law, not only new AI statutes.
- **Security and privacy statutes are in scope.** SHIELD Act safeguards and breach-related duties sit beside AI-specific disclosure rules, so AI deployments need the same data security evidence as any system.
- **Incident reporting.** RAISE's duty to report security and safety incidents mirrors the incident-reporting trend seen elsewhere in this hub, for example in the [Bridgewater policy table](bridgewater-ai-cyber-virus-2026.md).
- **Chatbots and children.** Several of the listed laws target consumer-facing chatbots, which matters for any company deploying them to New York users.
- **Technical expertise inside enforcers.** PYMNTS notes the unit's technical capacity and broad mandate, suggesting investigations beyond the frontier-model rules.
