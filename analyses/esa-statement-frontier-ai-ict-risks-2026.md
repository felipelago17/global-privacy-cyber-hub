# ESAs Statement JC 2026 25 — ICT risks from frontier AI models (31 July 2026)

`EU · Financial sector · DORA · Frontier AI · Cyber resilience · Supervision`

## Source

- **Title:** Toward a consistent and risk-based approach for ICT risks from frontier AI models
- **Issuer:** European Supervisory Authorities (EBA, EIOPA, ESMA) through the Joint Committee
- **Reference and date:** JC 2026 25, 31 July 2026
- **Type:** Supervisory statement; not a new legal requirement
- **Link:** <https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/JC_2026_25_ESA_statement_on_frontier_AI_models.pdf>

## Summary

The ESAs explain how existing EU rules apply to the cyber risks that advanced frontier AI models create for financial entities, and recommend mitigation. Their concern is that such models can speed up the discovery and exploitation of vulnerabilities, and can hit shared infrastructure and single points of failure across many entities at once, which makes the risk systemic. Defenders can use the same tools, but only through quality-assured protocols. The aim is coordinated supervision and a level playing field across the EU. The statement relies on the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and applies it proportionately.

## Context the statement cites

- European Commission Action Plan on Cybersecurity and AI, 7 July 2026.
- ESRB warning on systemic cyber risks from frontier AI models (ESRB/2026/3), 25 June 2026: multi-day or multi-institution disruption could reach the wider economy.
- ENISA's initial recommendations on operational capabilities for the frontier-AI era.
- An ECB message to CEOs of significant institutions to close open supervisory findings and ICT and security weaknesses without delay, including findings from on-site inspections, targeted reviews and the 2024 cyber-resilience stress test. Other competent authorities may set similar expectations.
- IMF blog of 7 May 2026 on financial-stability risks from AI-fuelled cyberattacks.

## Legal framework

- **DORA** stays fully relevant: ICT risk management, testing, incident and recovery management, and ICT third-party risk. Proportionality applies under DORA Article 4.
- **AI Act** obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk (transparency, technical documentation, cybersecurity measures) are noted.
- The framework is technology-neutral, but shorter discovery-to-exploitation cycles mean entities should act quickly and proactively.
- NIS2 is not mentioned in the statement.

## Expectations of financial entities

1. Act quickly and proactively on cybersecurity capability, proportionate to size, risk profile and complexity.
2. Adjust ICT risk processes and controls across three strategies: **prevention, detection and management**.
3. Set up governance for frontier-AI risk without delay, with committed management bodies, clear accountability, timely response plans and adequate investment.
4. Review the Risk Appetite Framework: metrics, tolerance thresholds and controls, covering both internal use of AI models and indirect exposure to them.

## Mitigation measures

| Area | Measures |
|---|---|
| Prevention | Current inventories of all IT assets including AI/ML components, classified by criticality and exposure; secure-by-design and automated security controls; protect source code and critical configurations; shrink the attack surface by removing exposures, segmenting networks and retiring legacy systems; proactive, automated patching for high-risk systems including disaster-recovery and backup systems, with testing and rollback; least privilege and MFA; enforce standards across providers, hardware and software vendors and open-source communities |
| Detection | Continuous rather than periodic vulnerability scanning and monitoring; comprehensive logging and behavioural monitoring with deception and lateral-movement detection, avoiding data overload; more frequent scans, tests and compliance checks; AI-assisted SOC and red-teaming |
| Management | Update incident reporting and business continuity for AI-assisted threats and multi-system failures; keep backups from sharing the risks of primary systems; test resilience against AI-enhanced scenarios; map dependencies (software, cloud, DNS, outsourced functions); continuous management-body oversight; automated risk monitoring and escalation; staff awareness and anomaly reporting |

## Third-party and ICT risk

- Entities should use dependency mapping and supply-chain assessments to prioritise preventive measures and discuss these risks with ICT third-party providers.
- As Lead Overseers, the ESAs engaged critical ICT third-party providers (CTPPs) on how they identify and manage these risks. The findings fed into the annual risk assessment and the 2027 Oversight Plan.
- AI-related risks are being added to the Oversight Examination Methodology and will appear in 2027 examinations of CTPP preparedness.

## Timeline

- **Immediately:** close outstanding supervisory findings; set up governance.
- **Ongoing:** the ESAs work with competent authorities on a proportionate approach.
- **2027:** AI-related threats built into CTPP oversight examinations.

## Why it matters

- It is the first joint ESA position on frontier-AI cyber risk and ties it to binding DORA duties, so supervisors can test firms against it even though the statement creates no new obligation.
- Dependency and asset inventories, patch speed and backup isolation are the practical points an examiner is likely to ask about.
- The CTPP angle matters for cloud and AI providers serving EU financial entities: their preparedness will be examined in 2027.
