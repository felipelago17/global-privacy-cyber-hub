# Saudi Arabia's draft Responsible AI Policy: from a guiding regime to a supervisory one (Clifford Chance, Nov 2026)

`Saudi Arabia · SDAIA · Responsible AI · Risk tiers · Pre-deployment review · Sandbox · Source: AS&H Clifford Chance (law firm briefing)`

Status as of 16 Nov 2026. This page summarises a law firm's client briefing, which is a secondary source describing a draft for public consultation. I did not read the SDAIA text itself, and the briefing cites no article numbers, so nothing below should be quoted as a provision of the policy.

## The source

AS&H Clifford Chance (Riyadh), "Responsible AI Policy: A Shift from a Guiding Regime to a Supervisory One", dated 4 November 2026: <https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2026/11/responsible-ai-policy-shift-guiding-regime-supervisory-one.pdf>. The body lists four authors from the Riyadh office; the footer names two other contacts who are not in the byline.

## What the briefing says changes

Saudi AI governance has so far rested mainly on voluntary guidelines and non-binding principles. The Saudi Data & AI Authority (SDAIA) has published a draft Responsible AI Policy (RAI Policy) for public consultation. The briefing says it would add binding obligations, accountability rules and supervisory and enforcement powers for SDAIA, moving AI compliance from best practice to a regulatory requirement. A separate draft AI-First Policy, also in consultation, is described as high-level and principle-based: it applies to the public sector and recommends principles for state-owned and private entities. The briefing reads the two together as aiming to encourage adoption while adding governance.

## Key features of the draft (as described)

| Area | What the briefing reports |
|---|---|
| Scope | Public, private and non-profit sectors and individuals, across the lifecycle from development to decommissioning. Phased: the first stage makes compliance mandatory for high-risk sectors and large companies, as SDAIA determines |
| Foundation | SDAIA's seven AI Ethics Principles (fairness, privacy and security, humanity, social and environmental benefit, reliability and safety, transparency and explainability, accountability) are embedded; data use is to align with the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) |
| Risk tiers | Four: Critical, High, Limited, Minimal or No Risk. Critical-risk deployment prohibited; higher tiers get stricter governance and monitoring; SDAIA maintains a general risk framework |
| Registration | All AI service providers must register products on an AI Governance Platform; government AI offices must register products with SDAIA; private entities may register and obtain an annually renewable certificate |
| AI Ethics Badges | Five levels (Aware, Adoptive, Confirmative, Assured, Visionary). High-risk systems need at least Assured; limited-risk at least Confirmative |
| Governance and oversight | Formal structures, defined roles, internal controls, documentation for traceability and audit; meaningful human oversight, especially for automated decisions; one or more responsible AI assessors |
| High-risk systems | Pre-deployment safety testing and a formal safety report (guardrails, test results across inputs and outputs, misuse scenarios, post-deployment monitoring). The briefing says deployment is prohibited until SDAIA has reviewed the reports and test results. Annual audit by an accredited third party |
| Roles | Responsibilities allocated to developers, operators, AI service providers, content creators, users and public entities by control and involvement |
| Sandbox | Supervised pre-market testing. Developers of high-risk systems and providers of AI safety certification would need a sandbox participation certificate before offering services |

## Why it matters for privacy and cyber teams

- **The gate is regulator review, not self-assessment.** A pre-deployment review step by the authority is closer to product approval than to the notify-after model of most data-protection regimes. For vendors serving Saudi customers, release timelines could depend on SDAIA's review.
- **Safety reporting overlaps with security work.** Misuse scenarios, guardrail testing and post-deployment monitoring are the same artefacts security and privacy teams already produce for red-teaming, DPIAs and incident readiness. One evidence set serving several regimes is a practical aim.
- **Data governance is tied to the PDPL.** The briefing says data quality, security and privacy duties align with the PDPL, so a weak PDPL programme is a likely weak point under the RAI Policy too.
- **Cross-border comparison.** The risk-tier and registration design resembles the direction of the EU AI Act, but the pre-deployment review and badge levels are distinctive on the briefing's description. The UAE approach to AI is still principle- and sector-led, so groups operating across the Gulf may face uneven expectations; I have not compared texts.

## Gaps and cautions

- No article numbers, dates, consultation close, or timeline for the phased rollout are given.
- "High-risk sectors", "large companies" and how tiers are assigned are left to SDAIA.
- Registration wording is inconsistent in the briefing: private entities "may" register, yet are told to prepare for "possible" registration, and sandbox participation is described both as a registration framework and a mandatory certificate for high-risk developers.
- The briefing says SDAIA would have enforcement powers but describes no penalties, sanctions or appeal route.
- The AI Governance Platform, the accredited auditors and the sandbox are not yet defined ("to be established").
- It is a firm's reading of a draft that may change. Check the SDAIA consultation text before relying on any detail.

## Practical steps the briefing suggests

Inventory and classify AI systems by likely risk tier; set up governance, accountability and human oversight; keep technical documentation and safety reports; check PDPL-compliant data use; plan for registration, badge applications or sandbox participation for high-risk systems; and fold AI governance into existing compliance, risk, cybersecurity and data-protection frameworks while the draft is open.
